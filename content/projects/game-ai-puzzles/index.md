## Overview

**Undergraduate Research Assistant — Game AI, Gettysburg College · Summer 2023 · Advisor: Prof. Todd W. Neller**

Research on using **Monte Carlo sampling and stochastic local-search** methods to generate and evaluate combinatorial puzzles at scale.

## Research Question

Can stochastic search methods reliably generate large numbers of *solvable, diverse, and well-calibrated* combinatorial puzzles — and how do we measure puzzle quality once we have them?

## Approach

- Investigated Monte Carlo sampling and stochastic local-search approaches for puzzle generation and evaluation.
- Optimized the generation and ranking algorithms to produce and score **more than 1,000,000 solvable puzzle instances**.
- Conducted **empirical analysis** of the generated puzzles to evaluate diversity and distinguish levels of puzzle difficulty.

## My Contribution

I worked on the algorithmic core: improving the sampling and local-search procedures, scaling the generation and ranking pipeline past a million instances, and designing the empirical analysis of diversity and difficulty.

## Results

A scaled puzzle-generation system and an empirical characterization of what makes generated puzzles diverse and how difficulty distributes across instances — a lesson in evaluating generative processes, not just their outputs.

## What This Motivated

This was my first taste of research as *empirical science*: formulating a question about a stochastic process, building the machinery to investigate it, and letting the data answer. It seeded my lasting interest in **evaluation and reasoning under uncertainty**.
